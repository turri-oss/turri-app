# The window

Everything the window offers, view by view. The [README](../README.md) has the short tour.

It is written as on macOS. On Linux the window keeps its window manager's title bar, with the menus below it; Ctrl takes ⌘'s place in every shortcut; Quit is in File; Finder is the file manager; and what is macOS's alone - the Dock's badge and menu, File > Open Recent, the Window menu's list of projects - is left out: the welcome screen's recent projects and the project switcher (Ctrl+Shift+P) do their work. The preview's terminal copies and pastes with Ctrl+Shift+C and Ctrl+Shift+V, as Linux terminals do, so Ctrl+C stays the shell's.

- **Start a new project** - on the landing page (or File > New Project…, ⌘N): name a folder and choose where it goes, then which models it runs on - turri's vendors as `turri init-config` offers them - each saying whether you are signed in to the provider its models come from. Not signed in, the sheet offers to sign in to it there and then, in the sign-in sheet, before the project is made. On Amazon Bedrock, whose models - every maker pi's catalogue has there: Anthropic and OpenAI, xAI, Meta, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek, Amazon's Nova and the rest - where they run - a geography, or your AWS region - and the two models themselves, picked from what Bedrock serves there: turri's own pair for Anthropic's and OpenAI's, the strongest to talk to and a cheaper one to code for the others, as turri offers them. A geography other than global and us says which `AWS_REGION` to set - `ap-northeast-1` for jp, say - as pi calls Bedrock in us-east-1 unless `AWS_REGION`, or the AWS profile signed in with, names another. A turri that knows any pair (after 0.1.9) writes it and refuses a model pi's catalogue does not have; an older one writes its own, and the app puts the pair picked in their place. turri writes its `config.turri.yaml` (`initConfig`, from its host entry), the project opens on those models, and the chat starts with what turri wrote - its models; the terminal's advice on signing in is left out - ready for turri to write the first \*\*\*plain with you. A folder of that name with files in it already is left alone; a project given up at the models takes its empty folder with it. Then turri doctor's checks run on it, the models asked too, to see that they answer with your credentials - a word each, so it costs a little: over the project as it opens, or, when turri cannot start on the models chosen - no key for them, most often - in the new-project sheet itself, which says so and what to do, and tries again once it is seen to. The folder and its config stay either way.
- **turri** - the conversation, on the right (⌘J), as wide as you drag its edge (double-click it for the width it starts at), hidden by the × in its header and brought back by the turri button at the right of the toolbar: turri writes the \*\*\*plain with you and regenerates the code from it. Replies stream in, tool calls show as what they do ("Edited backend.plain"), a pulse that ends is one line in the chat - what it was, how it ended, its time and cost - coloured by its kind as in the work view, and a click opens it there, its report and \*\*\*plain with it, and when turri asks you something - `ask_user`, a confirm, a choice - it is docked at the foot of the chat in the message box's place, the options as buttons, and the conversation stays in view above it. While a skill of several phases runs - brood's intent, stack, testing and product - its phases are above the message box: the ones done ticked, the one it is in turning, and what that phase does. Type `/` for its commands. What turri says is Markdown, shown as it is written - lists, headings, tables, code - a `plain` block, or one with no language that has a \*\*\*plain section in it, coloured as the intent view colours the \*\*\*plain, a `diff` as a pulse's is in work, and the \*\*\*plain in a diff of a `.plain` file by its grammar too - links (to the browser), its escapes; raw HTML in it stays text. Under the message box, on the left, how full the conversation's context is - a ring and its percent, the tokens on hover - and what the conversation has cost so far (its own model's calls; the pulses' cost is in work). The ⟳ in its header starts a new session: the conversation over, nothing of the last one carried into it, as `/new` is in the terminal - not while pulses run, as they belong to the conversation's work loop. turri knows it is in the app, not a terminal: it is given the window's views - intent, work, preview - in place of the terminal's side panel and keys, and told at each turn which view and file you have open.

- **Files** - the whole project as a tree on the left: the \*\*\*plain files, each with its state and how much of it is built, and everything beside them - the templates they import, test scripts, resources, readmes, skills, config. Folders are listed as they are opened and again when what is in them changes, and which are open is kept for each project; what turri keeps for itself (`plain_modules`, `dist`, `.turri`) is there, quieter. Any file that is not a \*\*\*plain opens as text - a `.plain` template coloured by turri's grammar - to edit and save with ⌘S; what turri generates is read only, and a file that is not text opens in its own app. Right-click a row to open it elsewhere, reveal it in Finder or copy its path. Below the tree, the project's settings from `config.turri.yaml`; **debug** is a switch, off by default, that goes through the chat as `/turri-config debug true` - on, turri keeps what it writes for debugging it: every agent's whole log and session, each pulse's log (the work view's "open its log"), the test runs' outputs. Off, those go as each pulse ends, and the ledger is what is left of a pulse.
- **intent** - the `.plain` file, coloured by turri's own \*\*\*plain grammar, with each functional spec's state in the gutter beside it: a filled pink mark when its code is built, a cyan one with two ticks when its conformance tests are written and pass too - a step past built, and the \*\*\*plain's dot in the sidebar turns cyan when every functional spec is, a spinner while it is being built, an empty one while it is still to build - the \*\*\*plain said once, with how much of it is built above it. While a pulse runs, the lines it renders - what its \*\*\*plain change adds, or the functional spec it builds - are lit in the \*\*\*plain and brought into view. When turri edits the file from the chat it is taken in at once, or, over edits not yet saved, offered. Rest the pointer on a functional spec - any of its lines, or its mark - and its state pops up beside it: regenerating or conformance testing, with the pulse's stage and how long it has been at it, regenerated, regenerated and conformance tested, not regenerated yet, or new and not saved. Click a mark (a native menu) to regenerate that functional spec again or write its conformance tests - once it is built: one not built yet, new or changed, is regenerated, not again, with the others not built yet, as each is built on the ones before it, and its conformance tests wait for its code. Functional specs open collapsed to their first line, each showing how many lines it hides: click that, the line or the chevron beside its mark to open it, or Expand all to open them all - remembered for next time. The buttons go through the chat - Regenerate is `/regenerate backend.plain` - so there is one work loop, whoever asks. Regenerate (⌘R) asks how: on from where it stopped, for a module partly built - its functional specs not built yet, on top of what is, chosen first while there are any - what changed in the \*\*\*plain - the default, and \*\*\*plain > Regenerate What Changed (⇧⌘R) without asking - chosen built functional specs again though they did not change, the whole module from scratch, or it and every module it requires from scratch - with, for any of them, how to cut the work in your words. A module with nothing built yet has no code to start over or to regenerate again, and its functional specs are each built on the ones before: it is regenerated all of it, or with its whole chain from scratch when it requires others. From scratch deletes code, so turri asks in the chat first; the marks follow at once - the module's history moving, a pulse landing or its code deleted, has them read again.
- **work** - each codebase's plan, and every pulse live: its stage, what its agent is doing and for how long, what it cost. Each pulse is headed by its summary - ten words and 70 characters at most, as turri's planner is asked to write it, and cut to that if it is longer, the whole of it on hover. What the view says can be selected and copied (⌘C); each pulse's copy button, or a right-click on it, copies it for the chat - its summary, module, number, kind and id, which turri looks it up by - or its summary or id alone. Each pulse is coloured by its kind, as the intent view marks what it does: regenerate pink, conformance cyan - the planned ones too. A pulse that ended keeps its agents' report folded - Report, or Why it failed - to open and read in full; beside it, what the pulse found the \*\*\*plain does not say - its \*\*\*plain gaps, each with what it assumed meanwhile - folded the same way. Click a pulse to see the \*\*\*plain it works on - the \*\*\*plain change it renders, coloured as the \*\*\*plain is, its lines lit and numbered among the sections around them, or the functional spec a conformance run builds - in the panel's intent tab, and open in intent lights those lines in the intent view; its agents' conversation, as chat bubbles, is its other tab. Halting a pulse is turri's alone: the window has no Halt or Stop for one. When something fails that the pulse goes on past - its debugger or diagnosis, the model provider for good, a commit of its landed work - it is said once in a notice, naming the pulse, however often it fails, and marked on the pulse's card. Below this session's pulses are earlier sessions', from turri's ledger (`.turri/ledger/`), grouped by run - when it started, and how: in the app, in turri's terminal chat, a headless regenerate - the last three, and more on asking: each pulse's module, kind and number, how it ended - landed or not, who stopped it, or that its session stopped under it - how long it ran and its cost, and the same marks. They are read only. Click one for what the ledger says of it - as `turri ledger <pulse>` prints it: its rounds, test runs, verdicts, healers, what turri refused, its history - and its log, to open. Ask turri about one in the chat: it looks it up by its id.
- **preview** - the app you are building, tried in a browser or a terminal - a switch in its bar, remembered for each project (⌘3). The browser is a pane of the window: type its address - `localhost:8000`, or just `8000` - and use it as in a browser, with back, forward, reload and its developer tools. Each project starts with a fresh browser - no cookies, storage or cache of another project's, or of a session before - and nothing loaded: the address it had last is offered, to open with a click, as another project's app may answer there now. The page loads again when a pulse lands. turri sees and tries it too: the chat has browser tools on the same pane - open a page of the app, read its text and controls, read its console, click, type and send a form, take a screenshot - and the window comes to the preview while it uses them, so you watch it work. They go only to this machine's own servers (localhost), never the web, and exist only in the app. A screenshot costs more than reading the page, so only the newest is sent to the model as an image; a model that turns images down - some do that their catalogue says take them - is sent none from then on, and turri carries on in the same turn without it. The terminal is a shell in the project's folder - your login shell, with your PATH - for a CLI: type in it as in any terminal, coloured in turri's palette, and ⟳ in the bar starts a new one; it is the same shell however often you switch away and back. turri has terminal tools on it too - run a command, type into what runs or press a key (enter, ctrl-c), read what it shows - and the window comes to the terminal while it uses them. turri chooses which of the two to try something in by what it is: a CLI in the terminal, a frontend in the browser, a backend in the browser when it serves API docs to try it in (`/docs`, OpenAPI) and with curl in the terminal otherwise, anything else as it shows best; a server it starts in the terminal runs on there, for the browser to open.
- **Preview the plan** (\*\*\*plain menu) - `turri glimpse`: the pulses Regenerate would run, and what the coding agent would be told, before anything starts.
- **Release** - beside Regenerate, once something of the \*\*\*plain is built: it asks turri in the chat to release it - the module's last commit, exported to `dist/<module>` and started on this machine - open it in the preview, in the terminal or the browser as suits it, and then say how to open it and what to test, functional spec by functional spec.
- **Check This Machine…** (Help menu) - `turri doctor`'s checks, from turri's host entry (`doctor`), run by the window's engine - so on the PATH turri runs with in the app, which is not always your shell's: Node, bash, git, the OS sandbox (Seatbelt on macOS, bubblewrap on Linux) and a model provider; with a project open, also each module's toolchains and what its testing scripts run. What wants doing comes first, each with what to do about it. **ask the models too** sends each model `config.turri.yaml` names a word, to see that it answers with your credentials - a request each, so it costs a little, and only on the click. Under what is missing, the way to it for someone who has never opened a terminal: on macOS, **install Apple's developer tools** - Apple's own installer, which brings git, bash's tools and a C compiler (`xcode-select --install`); on Linux, git's and bubblewrap's pages; a module's toolchain - Python, Node, Java, Go, Rust and the rest turri's stacks name - its makers' download page, in the browser; no model provider, the sign-in sheet. Installed something, **check again**; a tool still missing wants turri started again, as it reads your shell's PATH as it starts. The machine is checked when the welcome screen opens and the project when it opens, the models not asked: anything missing is said there, in amber - on the welcome screen under its buttons, in a project at the foot of the sidebar - and a click opens the checks. A turri without `doctor` (0.1.9 and before) says it cannot check.
- **Getting started** - until a project has been opened, just installed, the welcome screen leads the way to the first in three steps, each ticked once done: this machine has what turri needs - git, bash and the OS sandbox, what is missing with where to get it, checked again as you come back to the window - turri can reach a model, signed in to a provider, and your first project, started. Help > Getting Started shows them again, in a sheet over a project.
- **Project settings** (the gear beside the project's name, File > Project Settings…) - every setting of the project's `config.turri.yaml`, as turri's `/turri-config` knows them: the models and how hard each thinks, chosen from those turri can reach here, conformance tests and their budget, review, mutation testing, releasing after every pulse, the testing scripts, how many pulses run at once and how big each is, turn limits, the agents' environment, debug, what the project keeps - and, folded away, each role's model. Each says what it does and when a change takes effect, and is found by typing in its filter; a set one can be reset to its default. A change is sent to turri as you would type it - `/turri-config review false` - so turri checks it, writes it into the file with its comments kept, and says in the chat when it takes effect; the sheet reads the file again once it has changed. Open config.turri.yaml takes you to the file itself. On a turri that does not give them all - 0.1.12 and before - the sheet has the six the sidebar used to show.
- **Settings** (turri > Settings…, File on Linux, ⌘,) - saved as you change them, for every window: the colours and the text size (as View's), the models the new-project sheet has chosen when it opens - or ask each time - whether the app looks for its updates on its own and whether pre-releases count, with where they are and **check now**, which turri comes with the app, and what it sends. What a project runs on stays its `config.turri.yaml`'s.
- **About turri** (turri menu, Help on Linux) - the app's version and turri's, Electron's, Chromium's and Node's; **privacy**, what turri sends and where, also Help > What turri Sends, and Where… ([privacy](privacy.md) says the same); and the **third-party notices**, Help > Third-Party Notices…: every package the app carries, each with its licence, its text a click away and searchable, and Chromium's licences in a page of their own.
- **Sign In to Model Providers…** (File menu) - pi's sign-ins, as `/login` runs them in turri in a terminal, in a sheet of the window's: every model provider pi knows - forty or so - searchable, the ones this project's models come from first, then those with a subscription to sign in with, then API keys. Each says whether it is connected and how: signed in, an API key saved, from an environment variable (it names it), from pi's `models.json`. Choose one: a provider with a subscription and API keys both asks which - sign in with your Claude Pro/Max, say, or use an API key. A subscription signs in in your browser, which opens on its own: the sheet shows the address too, to copy or open when it does not, waits for the browser, and offers a field for the code the browser ends on, should it not reach the app - whichever comes first wins. The page the browser ends on is turri's, with its logo and colours: signed in, or why not, in the provider's words. GitHub Copilot gives a code to type on its page: the sheet shows it, and a button copies it and opens the page. An API key is a password field; Amazon Bedrock and Google Vertex ask first how - their choices as buttons. Cancel stops a sign-in at any point. What a sign-in stores is kept in pi's `auth.json` (`~/.pi/agent/`), where the turri CLI keeps it, for every project; one stored can be signed out of, while a key from the environment or `models.json` stays - it is not the app's to remove. With a project open, the sign-in goes through its chat's own model runtime, so the conversation has the credential at once - and a chat that could not start on `harness-model` without it is put on it. The sheet opens from the welcome screen (sign in to your models…) and the doctor's sheet too; `/login` typed in the chat opens it - `/login anthropic` on that provider - rather than going to the model as words, and turri is told to point you there. When a new project's models have no credentials, the new-project sheet offers to sign in to each of their providers, and opens the project again once you have.
- A window for each project, as many open as you like, each with its own engine, work loop, chat, preview and terminal. A project opens in the window in front when it has none yet - the welcome screen - and in a window of its own otherwise; one open already comes to the front instead, never in two windows at once. ⇧⌘N opens a new window. ⇧⌘P drops a list of the open projects from the top of the window, the one you were in last first - selected, so ⇧⌘P and enter go back to it: type to filter them by name or folder, and enter, or a click, brings that project's window forward. Or go from one to another as with any Mac app: the Window menu lists them by project, ⌘` goes to the next, and the Dock lists them too.
- **Updates** - the app updates itself from its releases: a new one downloads on its own, a notification says when it is ready, and turri > Restart to Update (Help on Linux) restarts into it - or it is installed when the app next quits; while pulses run, restarting asks first. turri > Check for Updates… asks now. In the settings, the app can be kept from looking on its own - then only Check for Updates… looks - and take pre-releases too. turri comes with the app, so a new turri arrives this way too. A build not signed with a Developer ID on macOS, or the app run from source, says it cannot update itself.
- Native menus and shortcuts (⌘N, ⇧⌘N, ⇧⌘P, ⌘O, ⌘S, ⌘R, ⇧⌘R, ⌘1-3, ⌘J, and ⌘+ ⌘− ⌘0 to zoom the text, remembered), each acting on the window in front, recent projects - on the welcome screen too, each folder once by its full path, searched and scrolled past five - a dock badge counting the pulses running in every window, progress on each window while its pulses run, and a notification, naming the project, when one lands while its window is in the background - click it to bring that window forward.
- turri's look: turri-dark by default, turri-light or the system's as chosen (View > Appearance). Every word the window shows can be selected and copied (⌘C), as in a document - but for a button's label, as a click presses it. the system's own fonts, nothing bundled: San Francisco for the interface and the ***plain, and Menlo - SF Mono where it is installed - for code, diffs and the terminal; rounded corners, fine lines and soft shadows. \*\*\*plain's own mark - its three asterisks, in its green (#79FC96, `public/plain/plain-mark.svg`) - beside a `.plain` file's name, in the file tree and where the intent view and a pulse name it - and the name itself, wherever the window writes it, is the mark then plain: in its own words and in turri's in the chat, though not in code, nor in the menus and tooltips, which hold only text. turri's pixel jellyfish, from its pixel kit (`src/pixels/`, `public/jellyfish/`): on the welcome screen and in the empty chat it grows from a polyp, sinks and grows again, then sways; small, its logo mark sits in the sidebar and the chat, and the mascot bobs there while turri works. The app's icon is its logo mark.
